\begin{abstract}
Robot-learning datasets increasingly combine recorded actions, multimodal observations,
reconstructed scenes, and simulator exports, yet the semantic relationship between an episode and
the world in which it occurred is usually implicit. This creates hidden methodological failures:
trajectory replay drifts when command timing and frame semantics are underspecified, and evaluation
splits leak when episodes share reconstructed worlds, physical entities, or asset lineage. We
introduce \emph{WorldEpisode}, a formal, storage-neutral semantic contract that binds robot-learning
episodes to immutable world revisions through five coupled graphs: identity, frame/clock,
representation-role, temporal event/state, and provenance/derivation. The contract is not a
replacement for LeRobotDataset, Rerun, NCore, MCAP, OpenUSD, or glTF Gaussian splats; it specifies
the invariants those systems must preserve to support reproducible replay, conversion, and
evaluation. We implement executable round-trip binding artifacts, an active public
LeRobotDataset-to-WorldEpisode-to-LeRobotDataset conversion, a semantic validator, an independent
fixture set, and controlled experiments. Across seven bindings, native containers preserve only
17--39\% of the semantic contract, while WorldEpisode sidecars recover the complete contract for
dataset/log/world bindings. On a pinned public SO-101 LeRobotDataset v3 episode, the active
converter preserves 303 six-dimensional action rows, state rows, sample timestamps, two video
timestamp ranges, and derived physical-frame records with zero measured numerical loss while
explicitly reporting source-absent camera extrinsics, action units, and latency semantics. The
validator detects all 14 injected fault classes and all hand-authored independent fixture failures.
A real LeRobot trajectory reveals a four-frame, 133 ms effective action delay; timestamp-aware
alignment reduces validation joint RMSE from 4.732 to 1.862 degrees, and tested MuJoCo replay drops
from 3.425 to 1.563 degrees while the Isaac adapter remains ready but untested. On a public
ArmnetBench LeRobot release, random episode splitting leaks all tested world lineages and gives a
Torch behavioral-cloning baseline 0.850 offline imitation success, while a scene-disjoint split has
zero lineage leakage and drops to 0.000. These results show that world--episode semantics are not
documentation overhead; they are measurable controls for robot-learning validity.
\end{abstract}
