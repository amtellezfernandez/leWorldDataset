\begin{abstract}
Robot-learning datasets standardize observation--action trajectories, while 3D scene formats
standardize visual and physical assets. However, the correspondence between an episode and the
world in which it occurred remains encoded through dataset-specific assumptions, preventing reliable
reconstruction, replay, conversion, counterfactual augmentation, and leakage-safe evaluation. We
introduce \emph{WorldEpisode}, a storage- and representation-neutral interchange profile that binds
robot-learning episodes to immutable world revisions through explicit clocks and coordinate frames,
persistent entity identity, fully specified action semantics, multi-representation asset roles,
interaction events, provenance, and uncertainty. WorldEpisode does not replace existing containers
or scene formats; it provides bindings for LeRobotDataset, Rerun, NCore, MCAP, OpenUSD, glTF
Gaussian splats, and simulator-specific world targets. We implement a draft validator and
deterministic pilot suite that measures semantic retention across seven bindings, injects fourteen
physical-coherence faults, compares declared-latency replay against naive command-time replay, tests
lineage-safe split leakage, and evaluates a synthetic counterfactual-augmentation task. The pilot
suite detects all injected fault classes, improves replay RMSE by \(3.6\times\) over naive timing,
and exposes perfect leakage in random episode splits. These results do not replace large real-robot
evaluation, but they establish that the proposed contract is executable and that its missing
semantics affect measurable outcomes.
\end{abstract}
