\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{keybox}{Summary}
\textbf{The problem.} Interactive spatial datasets can pass every local loader and still be
behaviorally wrong: joint values in an undeclared unit, command timestamps replayed as
motor-effective time, train and test sharing the same reconstructed scene, a dropped collision role,
or an undeclared clock offset. We call this \emph{silent state drift} --- the bytes are readable, but
the state assumptions that make replay, training, or deployment meaningful have drifted.\par
\textbf{Why it goes uncaught.} LeRobotDataset, Rerun, MCAP, NCore, OpenUSD, and glTF each store a
piece, but none bind an episode to an immutable world revision with persistent identity, frame and
clock mappings, action-timing, representation roles, and provenance. The file validates; the contract
does not exist.\par
\textbf{Why it matters.} The result is weeks of wasted training, benchmark scores inflated by scene
leakage, and real-to-sim gaps blamed on the simulator rather than the data contract.\par
\textbf{What this paper contributes.} \emph{WorldEpisode}, an executable sidecar contract of those
invariants for robot-learning datasets: a validator and a one-line preflight that turn silent
failures into hard, auditable errors before training or deployment.
\end{keybox}

Imagine training a behavioral-cloning policy for three weeks on a public robot dataset. Every
Parquet, video, and scene file passes its local loader and the simulator reports an 85\% success
rate; deployed on the real arm, the policy immediately misses the object. Nothing was corrupt: the
dataset encoded joint values in a unit the controller did not declare, the replay script treated
command timestamps as motor-effective timestamps, and the random split let the policy see the same
reconstructed room in both training and evaluation. We call this failure class \emph{silent state
drift}. The same pattern appears outside robotics as well; Section~\ref{sec:limits} returns to that
point as future work, since this paper's measured evidence is entirely robotics.
Table~\ref{tab:failure-motivation} makes the stakes concrete: each row is a file that still loads,
the behavior it silently breaks, and the requirement that catches it. The rest of the paper turns
each requirement into an executable rule and measures what happens when it is missing.

Requirement IDs follow \texttt{AREA.number}: \texttt{ACTION} is the action-channel contract (units,
frame, command/effective timing), \texttt{TIME}/\texttt{FRAME} are clocks and spatial frames,
\texttt{REP} is representation role, \texttt{ASSET} is asset identity and digest, \texttt{SPLIT} is
evaluation-split lineage, \texttt{WORLD}/\texttt{TRACE} are the world revision and its episode
deltas, \texttt{PROV}/\texttt{QUALITY} are provenance and uncertainty, and \texttt{DATASET} is
corpus scale. Section~\ref{sec:questions} defines all of them. Robotics is the profile implemented
and evaluated here because it is the deepest current stress test: real hardware, asynchronous
control, multi-camera data, reconstructed worlds, simulator exports, and policy evaluation.

Existing formats solve important pieces of this chain. LeRobot vertically integrates robot learning
from motor interfaces through dataset collection, policies, and deployment~\citep{lerobot};
LeRobotDataset v3 stores high-frequency states, actions, and timestamps in Parquet, visual streams
in MP4 shards, and episode indexing in metadata~\citep{lerobot_v3}, and is the container this paper
evaluates in depth with active experiments on real downloaded data. Rerun provides a physical-data
layer for multimodal recordings, NCore documents explicit pose graphs and sensor conventions, and
OpenUSD, SimReady, glTF, and simulator-native formats describe composed worlds and assets, with
Gaussian splats entering standard containers through glTF's \texttt{KHR\_gaussian\_splatting}
extension~\citep{khronos_gs, gltf_gs}; these are covered as lighter binding-retention pilots in
Section~\ref{sec:evidence}. Recent real-to-sim systems such as RoboSnap and DROID-Sim show that
Gaussian-splat environments can support trajectory replay, synthetic data generation, and policy
evaluation at dataset scale~\citep{robosnap}. That progress makes the missing contract more urgent
rather than less: a layered scene can be visually faithful while dropping an object's collision
role, and a replayable trajectory is still wrong if the action vector is interpreted under a
different controller contract at deployment time.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Documenting datasets is not new. Datasheets for Datasets~\citep{gebru2021datasheets} and model cards
standardize prose descriptions of provenance and intended use; MLCommons
Croissant~\citep{croissant2024} adds a machine-readable metadata layer for ML-ready datasets; and
Open X-Embodiment~\citep{openx} aggregates robot data under a shared schema. Evaluation leakage is a
well-documented driver of the reproducibility crisis in ML-based science~\citep{kapoor2023leakage}.
WorldEpisode differs in three ways. First, it binds these fields to an \emph{immutable,
content-addressed world revision} with closed identity, frame/clock, role, and provenance graphs, so
cross-container agreement is checkable rather than merely described. Second, it is \emph{executable
and fail-closed}: a one-line preflight blocks a native LeRobot or Rerun artifact that cannot prove
the contract, and every conversion emits a machine-readable loss report. Third, it treats
\emph{lineage-disjoint evaluation splits} as a first-class requirement with measured consequences,
not as optional documentation. It is a contract and validator layered on existing containers, not a
replacement format or a dataset card.

WorldEpisode is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{storage-neutral}: serializable through LeRobotDataset v3, Rerun, NCore, MCAP, game telemetry, AV logs, or a reference package;
  \item \textbf{representation-neutral}: compatible with splats, meshes, NeRFs, point clouds, collision proxies, sensor streams, and future assets;
  \item \textbf{runtime-neutral}: mappable to MuJoCo, Isaac Sim, SAPIEN, Genesis, game engines, AV replay systems, and future runtimes;
  \item \textbf{loss-explicit}: conversions may be lossy, but never silently lossy.
\end{itemize}

The empirical claims in this release are intentionally bounded: they demonstrate executable failure
mechanisms and auditable conversion semantics, not a completed multi-lab benchmark, production game
engine audit, autonomous-driving fleet audit, or real-robot deployment study.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{WorldEpisode}, an executable state-integrity contract for robot-learning datasets,
  expressed as five graphs of invariants (identity; frames and clocks; representation roles; temporal
  state and events; provenance and lineage), with a JSON Schema, a semantic validator, and a
  one-line fail-closed preflight for native LeRobot and Rerun artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Loss-explicit binding}: round-trip conversions that emit machine-readable loss reports,
  with measured native-versus-sidecar semantic retention.
  \item \textbf{Controlled failure studies}: scene-lineage leakage, action-timing replay drift, and
  real-to-sim contract drift, plus two non-robotics pilots --- each an executable mechanism, with
  every stronger claim bounded by an explicit open reproduction gate.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reader's guide.} Section~\ref{sec:questions} states the contract --- five coupled graphs
and two corpus-level obligations, each mapped to requirement IDs and to the case that tests it.
Section~\ref{sec:contract} delivers the contract as an executable sidecar. Section~\ref{sec:evidence}
is the empirical core in three acts: what drift costs, what the contract catches, and how the audit
scales. Section~\ref{sec:positioning} positions the result, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} states the
boundary of every claim.
