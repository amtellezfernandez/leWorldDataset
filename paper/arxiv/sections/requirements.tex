\section{Requirements}

A portable world layout format should satisfy the following requirements.

\paragraph{Simulator agnostic.}
The document should not name a physics engine as its source of truth. Simulator adapters may
consume and transform the document, but the authored layout should remain independent of the
target engine.

\paragraph{Asset portable.}
All asset references should be relative to the document package and free of machine-specific paths.
This permits delivery as a single JSON file, a folder, a URL-addressed package, or a registry
artifact.

\paragraph{Frame explicit.}
Frame convention should be declared or mapped. Silent assumptions about whether a world follows
ROS REP-103~\citep{rosrep103}, Z-up, or an authoring-tool convention create transfer bugs that are
difficult to diagnose.

\paragraph{Appearance and physics separated.}
Visual assets and simulator collision geometry are related, but they are not the same contract.
A Gaussian splat may be valid as a perception or rendering asset while requiring a primitive or
mesh proxy for physics. A high-resolution mesh may require a simplified collision body.

\paragraph{Static first, scenario ready.}
Static layouts should be simple. Task randomization, success checks, policies, and evaluation
metrics should live in a scenario layer that references the layout.

\paragraph{Schema validatable.}
Third-party tools should validate documents without importing a full application. The schema should
be public and stable enough to serve as a norm.
