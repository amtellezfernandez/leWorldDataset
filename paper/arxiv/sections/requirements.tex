\section{The Contract: Five Graphs and Two Corpus Obligations}
\label{sec:questions}

Any valid spatial state must answer a practical question: what must be known before it can be
safely converted, replayed, synchronized, augmented, or used for evaluation? WorldEpisode answers
it with five coupled graphs at the level of a single episode record, two corpus-level obligations
that follow from those graphs, and one scale requirement for production datasets.
Table~\ref{tab:contract-map} maps each question to the structure that answers it, the requirement
IDs that make it checkable, and the case in Section~\ref{sec:evidence} that tests it.
WorldEpisode specializes these requirements for robot-learning episodes.

\subsection{The five coupled graphs}
\label{sec:invariants}

Figure~\ref{fig:five-graph-model} shows the five graphs that make a spatial state replayable and
auditable rather than just stored. Making them mutually referential is what turns a silent error
into a detectable one: an identity, timing, role, event, or lineage error becomes a broken
reference instead of a hidden assumption.

\paragraph{World revision and deltas (\texttt{WORLD.001}, \texttt{TRACE.001}).}
Each record references one immutable, content-addressed world revision plus an ordered list of
deltas; any change to geometry, scale, calibration, collision proxies, physical parameters, or
object identity creates a new revision. Content hashes let two datasets prove they refer to the
same world without relying on filenames.

\paragraph{Identity graph (\texttt{ENTITY.001}).}
Every physical or logical entity receives a persistent identifier, and the same
\texttt{entity\_id} must be used by segmentation masks, Gaussian groups, meshes, collision bodies,
simulator actors, language references, and interaction events. If a language command says ``pick up
the mug,'' the perception mask, the simulator object, and the collision proxy must identify the
same mug.

\paragraph{Frame and clock graphs (\texttt{FRAME.001}, \texttt{TIME.001}, \texttt{ACTION.002}).}
Every spatial value declares its frames, units, transform direction, and validity interval,
consistent with and extending ROS conventions~\citep{rosrep103}; every stream declares a clock
domain, timestamps, synchronization mapping, and estimated drift, with the rigor NCore demonstrates
for pose graphs~\citep{ncore}. Actions declare command time and effective motor time rather than
assuming those are the same instant. This graph turns a suspicious replay from ``the policy is
bad'' into a diagnosable offset, transform-direction, or unit error.

\paragraph{Representation-role graph (\texttt{REP.001}).}
One entity can have many representations (splat, mesh, point cloud, collision proxy, mass/inertia
record), each declaring a role and a digest-verified asset descriptor. The role field is essential:
a splat may be valid for appearance but invalid for collision, while a simplified proxy may be
valid for collision but unsuitable for visual training.

\paragraph{Temporal-event graph (\texttt{TRACE.001}).}
Records store interaction events --- contact, grasp, attachment, collision patch, reset,
intervention, failure --- each referencing entities, time, confidence, and source, turning raw
coordinate streams into auditable milestones.

\paragraph{Provenance graph (\texttt{PROV.001}).}
Every derived asset identifies its source episodes, sensors, reconstruction algorithm and version,
calibration revision, hashes, creator, license, and quality metrics, mapping to existing provenance
vocabularies rather than inventing an incompatible one.

The full normative field lists are specified in the v0.1 RFC and its JSON Schemas; the paper keeps
only what each graph must guarantee.

\subsection{Two corpus-level obligations, plus scale}

\paragraph{Loss-explicit conversion (\texttt{CONVERT.001}).}
WorldEpisode reuses existing standards rather than replacing them. USD~\citep{usd} carries composed
simulation worlds, glTF carries Gaussian appearance assets~\citep{gltf_gs}, NCore carries capture
and pose-graph conventions~\citep{ncore}, Rerun carries physical-data recordings with column
chunks~\citep{rerun}, ROS/MCAP carries logs, and domain-native engines carry their own telemetry.
A converter may externalize or approximate fields, but it must emit a machine-readable loss report
instead of silently discarding semantics. The standard permits lossy conversion; it forbids silent
lossy conversion.

\paragraph{Lineage-safe splits (\texttt{SPLIT.001}).}
Record-level random splits are not enough when multiple records share a reconstructed room, map
patch, physical object, source capture, calibration, generated asset lineage, or state ancestry ---
exactly what the provenance graph records. A WorldEpisode split manifest can therefore enforce
disjointness by constraints such as \texttt{world\_lineage}, \texttt{physical\_entity},
\texttt{source\_capture}, \texttt{reconstruction\_run}, or \texttt{generated\_asset\_family}.

\paragraph{Scale (\texttt{DATASET.001}).}
Large robot corpora require a dataset manifest above the episode records: namespaces, resolver
priority, shard catalogs, materialized indexes, split catalogs, and append-only versions. This lets
a reader locate all SO-101 pick-place traces for a world-disjoint split by index lookup rather than
by crawling storage paths.

The initial WorldEpisode profile is intentionally narrow: rigid tabletop manipulation with
fixed-base single- or dual-arm robots. That bounded domain keeps conformance executable rather than
aspirational. Future profiles can extend this specification to humanoids, locomotion, deformables,
fluids, multi-agent game worlds, and autonomous-driving logs.
