\section{Evidence: What the Audit Finds}
\label{sec:evidence}

We test what drift changes (Cases 1--3), what the contract detects or preserves (Cases 4--6), and
which controls remain unavailable in \ExpBenchmarkCount{} public benchmarks (Case 7). All measured data are
robotics. Appendix~\ref{app:boundaries} records claim boundaries and Appendix~\ref{app:gates}
records unexecuted gates.

\subsection{What drift costs}

\paragraph{Case 1: task--scene proxy leakage in evaluation splits (\texttt{SPLIT.001}).}
We audit \ExpSceneEpisodeCount{} teleoperated episodes from ArmnetBench v0.1's SO-101 LeRobot
release~\citep{armnetbench_lerobot_so101}. Public metadata does not expose physical scene IDs, so we
hash source revision, robot type, task ID/text, and camera schema into
\ExpSceneLineageCount{} \emph{task--scene proxy} lineages. A seeded
\ExpSceneRandomTrainPercent/\ExpSceneRandomTestPercent{} episode split
(\ExpSceneRandomTrainEpisodes/\ExpSceneRandomTestEpisodes{} episodes) puts every test proxy in
training. The comparison split holds out task IDs \ExpSceneHeldoutTaskIndices{}
(\ExpSceneHeldoutTrainEpisodes/\ExpSceneHeldoutTestEpisodes{} episodes), yielding
\ExpSceneHeldoutOverlap{} proxy overlap.
\emph{This is also a task-disjoint shift}; it cannot identify the causal effect of scene overlap.

A \ExpMlpLayerCount-layer Torch MLP (\ExpMlpHiddenUnits{} units/layer, AdamW,
\ExpMlpEpochCount{} epochs, \ExpMlpSeedCount{} seeds) maps state, episode phase, timestamp, and task
one-hot features to action. The primary metric, mean episode nRMSE, changes from
\ExpMlpRandomNrmse{} ($\pm\ExpMlpRandomNrmseSeedStd$ seed SD;
\ExpConfidencePercent\% crossed seed--episode bootstrap CI
[\ExpMlpRandomNrmseCiLow, \ExpMlpRandomNrmseCiHigh]) to \ExpMlpHeldoutNrmse{}
($\pm\ExpMlpHeldoutNrmseSeedStd$;
[\ExpMlpHeldoutNrmseCiLow, \ExpMlpHeldoutNrmseCiHigh]); the increase is
\ExpMlpNrmseDifference{} [\ExpMlpNrmseDifferenceCiLow, \ExpMlpNrmseDifferenceCiHigh].
As a secondary diagnostic, the rate below a fixed \ExpOfflineSuccessThreshold{} nRMSE threshold is
\ExpMlpRandomSuccessRate{} ($\pm\ExpMlpRandomSuccessSeedStd$ seed SD) versus
\ExpMlpHeldoutSuccessRate{} ($\pm\ExpMlpHeldoutSuccessSeedStd$). This threshold was not selected on
a validation set and is not task success. Task one-hot features make this probe particularly
sensitive to the task shift.

A deterministic ridge policy using only a \ExpTemporalHistoryFrames-frame state history has mean
episode nRMSE \ExpTemporalRandomNrmse{}
[\ExpTemporalRandomNrmseCiLow, \ExpTemporalRandomNrmseCiHigh] versus
\ExpTemporalHeldoutNrmse{} [\ExpTemporalHeldoutNrmseCiLow,
\ExpTemporalHeldoutNrmseCiHigh]; the increase is \ExpTemporalNrmseDifference{}
[\ExpTemporalNrmseDifferenceCiLow, \ExpTemporalNrmseDifferenceCiHigh]. Its secondary thresholded
rates are \ExpTemporalRandomSuccessRate{}
(\ExpTemporalRandomSuccessCount/\ExpSceneRandomTestEpisodes{};
[\ExpTemporalRandomSuccessCiLow, \ExpTemporalRandomSuccessCiHigh]) versus
\ExpTemporalHeldoutSuccessRate{}
(\ExpTemporalHeldoutSuccessCount/\ExpSceneHeldoutTestEpisodes{};
[\ExpTemporalHeldoutSuccessCiLow, \ExpTemporalHeldoutSuccessCiHigh]); its independently bootstrapped
rate difference is \ExpTemporalSuccessDifference{}
[\ExpTemporalSuccessDifferenceCiLow, \ExpTemporalSuccessDifferenceCiHigh]. We use
\ExpBootstrapResamples{} resamples. MLP intervals cross optimization seeds with held-out episodes,
pair sampled seeds across protocols, and resample the different episode sets independently; ridge
intervals resample episodes only. Thus the experiment establishes that the random split is not
proxy-lineage-disjoint and that evaluation changes materially under a harder task--scene holdout;
it does not establish scene-only leakage, policy rollout impact, or benchmark inflation.

The artifact includes digest-verified split packages and ACT/Diffusion job specifications. A pinned
LeRobot \ExpPolicyCompatibilityLeRobotVersion{} preflight first loads the representative
low-dimensional package; \ExpPolicyCompatibilityTrainingStepCount{}/\ExpPolicyCompatibilityProbeCount{}
policy paths complete a training step, and all \ExpPolicyCompatibilityExpectedBlockerCount{} stop
because the package has neither an observation image nor an environment-state feature. We do not
relabel joint proprioception to bypass that contract. A subsequent generated plan pins
\ExpPolicyVisionAssetCount{} source front-camera files (\ExpPolicyVisionAssetGiB{} GiB) and
materializes split-specific video metadata and statistics for all \ExpPolicyVisionPackageCount{}
packages. With those inputs, \ExpPolicyVisionTrainingStepCount{}/\ExpPolicyVisionProbeCount{} pinned
LeRobot \ExpPolicyVisionLeRobotVersion{} ACT/Diffusion paths complete the CUDA smoke optimization step.
This closes input compatibility only: checkpoint training, held-out policy metrics, and rollouts
remain open (Appendix~\ref{app:gates}).

\paragraph{Case 2: action-timing and replay drift (\texttt{ACTION.002}).}
LeRobot's control stack can decouple policy inference from the fixed-rate low-level controller:
commands may be queued, chunked, interpolated, or consumed after policy output~\citep{lerobot}.
We estimate an integer action/state lag from \ExpTimingCalibrationEpisodes{} calibration
trajectories spanning \ExpTimingTaskCount{} tasks, freeze it, and test
\ExpTimingHeldoutEpisodes{} source-episode-disjoint trajectories with
\ExpTimingSourceEpisodeOverlap{} source-episode overlap.
The selected lag is \ExpTimingDelayFrames{} frames (\ExpTimingDelayMilliseconds{} ms). On identical
within-episode target rows, zero-delay alignment has pooled RMSE \ExpTimingZeroRmse{} source
position units and frozen-delay alignment has \ExpTimingFrozenRmse{}. The mean paired episode
improvement is \ExpTimingImprovement{} (\ExpConfidencePercent\% CI
[\ExpTimingImprovementCiLow, \ExpTimingImprovementCiHigh]); all
\ExpTimingImprovedEpisodeCount/\ExpTimingHeldoutEpisodes{} held-out trajectories improve.

This is an action/state telemetry-lag proxy, not measured motor latency. The source exposes
\ExpTimingTimestampColumnCount{} float32 frame-timestamp column, no motor-effective timestamps,
\ExpTimingRobotTypeCount{} robot type, and \ExpTimingControllerConfigurationCount{} controller
configuration.
Using the derived time shift, timestamp zero-order hold gives \ExpTimingTimestampZohRmse{} RMSE
whereas linear interpolation gives \ExpTimingTimestampLinearRmse{}, showing that the timestamp
comparison is interpolation-sensitive rather than independent queue evidence.

Separately, \ExpReplayTraceCount{} \ExpReplayTraceNoun{} has \ExpReplaySampleCount{}
\ExpReplayJointCount-joint samples at
\ExpReplayRateHz{} Hz; its calibration prefix selects \ExpReplayDelayFrames{} frames, or
\ExpReplayDelayMilliseconds{} ms, and its held-out suffix changes alignment RMSE from
\ExpAlignmentNaiveRmse{} to \ExpAlignmentTimedRmse{} degrees. A WorldEpisode contract declares the
estimated command/effective semantics, interpolation, and latency model. We execute it through
\ExpReplayTestedSimulatorCount{} minimal position-servo adapters: MuJoCo changes from
\ExpMujocoNaiveRmse{} to \ExpMujocoTimedRmse{} degrees, while Genesis same-trace replay changes
from \ExpGenesisNaiveRmse{} to \ExpGenesisTimedRmse{}. Their naive values
(\ExpMujocoNaiveRmsePrecise{} and \ExpGenesisNaiveRmsePrecise{}) are nearly identical because both
integrate the same minimal servo model, not because their physics agree. The Isaac adapter is
emitted as a ready mapping from the same contract, but is not tested in this environment.

A dependency-free replay-adapter conformance harness guards additional runtime adapters: across
\ExpAdapterConformanceCaseCount{} scheduling cases, including
constant frame delay, zero-order hold for missing commands, and asynchronous queue selection, a
naive scheduler fails while the contract-aware scheduler matches the expected trace with zero
numerical error (\ExpAdapterConformanceMaxError{} maximum RMSE). This validates scheduler and
same-trace adapter semantics only; it does not claim
contact-rich task replay, Isaac/SAPIEN execution, or simulator-independent
behavior.

\paragraph{Case 3: controlled real-to-sim contract drift (\texttt{ACTION.001}, \texttt{REP.001}).}
\ExpDriftAblationCount{} deterministic proxies isolate an absolute-radian versus delta-degree
controller mismatch and an appearance-only Gaussian export that drops collision geometry. Drifted
contracts produce \ExpDriftSimulationSuccessCount/\ExpDriftAblationCount{} proxy simulation
successes and
\ExpDriftDeploymentSuccessCount/\ExpDriftAblationCount{} deployment-proxy successes; explicit
action and representation-role contracts recover
\ExpDriftCorrectedSuccessCount/\ExpDriftAblationCount{}. This is a mechanism check, not a hardware
or RoboSnap rerun.

\subsection{What the contract catches}

\paragraph{Case 4: validator fault detection (all requirement IDs).}
We inject \ExpFaultCaseCount{} faults into the baseline package: missing clock domains, incomplete cross-clock
mappings, unknown frames, missing transform intervals, unknown event entities, missing
representation roles, digest errors, action-contract omissions, non-content-addressed worlds,
missing delta sequences, missing provenance, and missing quality records. The validator detects all
\ExpFaultTruePositiveCount{} expected requirement failures with \ExpFaultRecall{} recall and
\ExpFaultPrecision{} precision. \ExpIndependentFixtureCount{} hand-authored
independent fixtures also trigger the expected diagnostics. The single false-positive requirement is
useful rather than harmful: removing an action reference frame triggers both \texttt{ACTION.001} and
the downstream \texttt{FRAME.001} reference check. The same suite includes a unit sanity check from
\path{lerobot/svla_so101_pickplace}: the first mirrored SO-101 frame has
\ExpJointSampleCount{} joint values with maximum magnitude \ExpJointSampleMaxDegrees{} in degrees
and \ExpJointSampleMaxRadians{} after radian conversion, so treating those degree values as radians
would violate the action/state unit contract even though the tensor shape is valid.

A pilot natural-source audit records \ExpNaturalFindingCount{} source omissions or evaluation risks
across \ExpNaturalActiveDatasetCount{} active
LeRobot artifacts plus source-level DROID and BridgeData V2 metadata. These are not
maintainer-confirmed bugs or prevalence estimates. A \ExpPreflightCaseCount-case preflight regression
passes a valid sidecar and rejects an invalid manifest plus native LeRobot/Rerun artifacts that lack
sidecars. An internal reader independent of the SDK path detects all
\ExpCleanroomRequirementCount{} expected requirement instances across
\ExpCleanroomFixtureCount{} fixtures; this tests artifact readability, not external adoption.

\paragraph{Case 5: loss-explicit dataset conversion (\texttt{CONVERT.001}).}
To test an actual LeRobotDataset rather than only a capability model, we implement
\(\text{LeRobotDataset v3} \rightarrow \text{WorldEpisode} \rightarrow \text{LeRobotDataset v3}\)
on \ExpRoundtripBatchEpisodes-episode batches from \ExpRoundtripDatasetCount{} pinned public datasets:
\path{lerobot/svla_so101_pickplace}~\citep{lerobot_svla_so101_pickplace} and
\path{lerobot/pusht}~\citep{lerobot_pusht}. The converter downloads the real Parquet metadata and
data shards, builds a schema-valid WorldEpisode manifest and sidecar, exports a compact
LeRobotDataset v3 package, reads it back, and asserts exact equality. These
\ExpRoundtripEpisodeCount{} committed episode packages provide inspectable per-episode evidence.

For scale, we convert every episode assigned to one immutable source Parquet shard from each of
\ExpConversionScaleDatasetCount{} datasets: the two above and ArmnetBench
v0.1~\citep{armnetbench_lerobot_so101}. No episode-level sampling is used. Across
\ExpConversionScaleEpisodeCount{} episodes, including \ExpConversionScaleMultiCameraDatasetCount{}
multi-camera datasets, the round trip preserves \ExpConversionScaleRowCount{} source-native paired
action/state samples, indices, sample timestamps, and video timestamp ranges with maximum absolute
error \ExpConversionScaleMaxError{}. The \ExpConversionScaleSourceInputMiB{} MiB input produces
\ExpConversionScaleTemporaryOutputMiB{} MiB of temporary packages in
\ExpConversionScaleWallSeconds{} s with \ExpConversionScalePeakMemoryMiB{} MiB peak resident memory.
Source video payloads are neither downloaded nor converted; only their stream and timestamp metadata
are audited. This is a complete-selected-shard measurement, not full-corpus throughput.

Both audits preserve derived physical-frame records through the sidecar and do not fake missing
semantics. The same \ExpConversionScaleSemanticLossFieldCount{} source-absent fields---camera
extrinsics, robot/world calibration, action units, and controller latency---are reported as
conversion loss rather than guessed.

\paragraph{Case 6: binding retention (\texttt{CONVERT.001}).}
\ExpBindingCount{} pilot bindings are compared against a versioned \ExpBindingFieldCount-field
projection. Non-reference native retention ranges from \ExpBindingNativeMin{} to
\ExpBindingNativeMax{}; sidecars recover \ExpBindingLerobotSidecar{} for tested dataset/log/world
bindings by serializing the missing fields. This is a round-trip check and gap inventory, not a
universal format ranking; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:binding-retention} reports the per-binding values.

\subsection{Scaling the audit}

\paragraph{Case 7: source-level benchmark audit (\texttt{SPLIT.001}).}
We audit public metadata for Open
X-Embodiment/RT-X~\citep{openx}, DROID~\citep{droid}, BridgeData V2~\citep{bridgedata},
LIBERO~\citep{libero}, and CALVIN~\citep{calvin}. The audit asks whether public metadata exposes
lineage splits, content-addressed world revisions, persistent entity identity, action timing
contracts, frame/clock graphs, and loss reports. Each has at least one high-severity open control.
A DROID-100 proxy rerun over \ExpDroidRerunEpisodes{} episodes and \ExpDroidRerunFrames{} frames
scores \ExpDroidCorrectedScore{} under its corrected proxy split versus \ExpDroidRandomScore{}
randomly, so it provides no inflation evidence. The proof gate therefore records
\ExpBenchmarkInflationClaimCount{} famous-benchmark inflation claims.

The same audit protocol also runs above the episode level: a dataset-scale manifest audit and a
generated catalog benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:scale}) make the \texttt{DATASET.001} scale claim
executable on catalog-side evidence only.
